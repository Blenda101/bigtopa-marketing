Chapter 05 · Pillars · Adaptive

Fit

Tailored, not off the rack.

Do members have the right tools for this moment, no more and no less?

01 · What breaks without it

Too many tools overwhelm. Too few underwhelm.

Most platforms are built for everyone, which means they're built for no one in particular. Enterprise tools bury a small organization in features it will never use. Consumer apps miss what it needs, like dues, elections or member groups, so members fill the gaps with yet another app.

Off the rack doesn't fit anyone.

When members can't find what they need, or can't ignore what they don't, they stop opening the app. The goal is the middle: the right tools for this group, at this stage, on this screen.

Overwhelmed

Every feature, on every screen, for everyone.

Tailored

The right tools for this group, at this stage.

Underwhelmed

The tools members need are missing.

02 · What good looks like

A space that feels made for you.

Fit means the space adapts to its group at every level, the way a tailored suit is cut for one person:

  • The kind of groupThe tools, words and defaults match the kind of organization you are.
  • The spaceA committee's work space, a member community space, an event space and a one-on-one space for coaching or mentoring each show different tools.
  • The momentIn a one-on-one call, hand-raising disappears. In a webinar, it appears. The interface changes with the activity.
  • The relationshipAs members move from visitor to regular to leader, the space opens up to them.

Fit starts with the spaces you open

A group opens the spaces it actually needs, with rooms inside them, instead of inheriting someone else's layout. A youth-serving organization might open a Programs space with a room for each age group, a Members space, and a Foundation space with a room for its board. As the group changes, spaces and rooms are added or retired.

Layouts that fit the content

Fit applies to layout too. When a shelf or blog holds only a few items, it sits compactly beside the conversation. As it grows, it expands into a full browsing view. How much of something exists decides how much space it gets.

It keeps fitting

A group of 12 and a group of 400 need different spaces, and most groups change size over time. Fit adapts as the community grows and relationships deepen, so the suit still fits next year.

03 · How BigTopa delivers it

Fit is built into how every space is put together.

  • Spaces you chooseOpen the spaces and rooms your group needs, and add or retire them as it changes.
  • Space typesWork, community, event and provider spaces (one-on-one services such as coaching or mentoring), each with its own conversations, roles, meetups and tools.
  • Meetup typesInstant calls through to broadcasts, turned on or off for each space.
  • Adaptive layoutsShelves and blogs resize to how much they hold.
  • Space settingsTurn tools on or off for each space, and decide who can do what there.
  • Plans built from featuresPlans are assembled feature by feature, so a group gets exactly what it needs and nothing it doesn't.
  • Your vocabularyRoles, programs and events named in your group's own words.

04 · Ask yourself

Questions for your leadership team.

  1. Which features in your current tools has nobody used in the last six months?
  2. What do members still do in a separate app because your main tool can't?
  3. Does your committee space look different from your social space? Should it?
  4. How long does it take a brand-new member to do one useful thing?
  5. Has your setup changed since your group grew, or shrank?

05 · One signal to watch

Time to first contribution.

→

How long it takes a new member to do one meaningful thing after joining: RSVP, post, give or join a meetup. A space that fits shortens it. A space that overwhelms or underwhelms stretches it.
