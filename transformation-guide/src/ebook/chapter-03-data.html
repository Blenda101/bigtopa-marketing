Data

Members share what they trust you to protect.

Do members trust us with what we know about them?

01 · What breaks without it

Member data is scattered, stale and hard to trust.

Most organizationschapters keep member information in four or five places: a parent organization'snational roster, a spreadsheet from the last treasurer, a payment app, a group chat's member list and an event sign-up form. Each holds a different piece of the same person, and none of them agree. For organizations with chapters, local, regional and national records rarely line up.For graduate chapters, local, regional and national records rarely line up.

Members notice. They get the wrong name on an email, a dues reminder after they've paid, an invitation for a city they left years ago. And they're right to hesitate before sharing more: in a group chat, everyone can see their phone number.

02 · What good looks like

One trusted record, with members in control.

Healthy groups keep one record per member, built from the best of every source: the official membership details from the parent organizationnational, the dues history from the treasurer, and the member's own current phone number. Then they hand control of visibility to the member.

  • One record per memberDuplicates merged, with the most reliable source winning for each detail.
  • Members control visibilityName and photo are visible to verified members. Phone, email and address are each hidden by default and shared only if the member chooses.
  • Verified members onlyDirectories show real, verified members, with no anonymous accounts and no bulk exports of contact details.
  • Cleanup as a welcomeConsolidating member data happens as a guided step when your organizationchapter sets up its space, not a project nobody finishes.

Complete profiles are the first act of engagement. A real photo and a current city turn a roster into a community members can actually find. And completing a profile is not the same as publishing your life: members decide what's shared.
