Unification

Stop stitching tools together. Move into one home.

Are our people and tools in one place?

01 · What breaks without it

Every tool switch costs context.

Chat in one app, events in another, dues in a third, files in a fourth. Each was a reasonable choice on its own. Together they're a patchwork: members juggle a handful of logins, leadersofficers copy the same information between systems, and decisions made in one tool never reach the people in another.

Connecting the apps doesn't fix it. Integrations pass data around, but the experience still feels like five apps, because it is.

There's no such thing as a free app. A $0 tool still gets paid: with your organization'schapter's data, which lives on someone else's terms (03Data); with leaderofficer hours spent copying between systems; and with members who tune out or miss what matters because it lives somewhere else (07Feel). As you use the calculator below, count those costs too.

02 · What good looks like

One home, built in, not bolted on.

Healthy groups run their life from one place: conversations, meetings, events, files, giving and the member directory, all sharing the same members, the same permissions and the same history. Moving from a conversation to a meeting to a payment is a change of mode, not a change of app.

  • One loginMembers sign in once to everything the group does.
  • One member listEveryone, their roles and their permissions, in one place.
  • One historyA decision, the meeting it came from and the file it produced stay connected.
  • Native, not stitchedCore tools are built in. Outside connections, such as payment processing, are the exception, not the glue.

One hub for each groupchapter

The home has a name: the hub. Every groupchapter has one, and everything else lives inside it: ongoing spaces for its main areas, smaller rooms inside them, and meetups that come and go. In organizations with local chapters, the national organization, each region and each chapterYour national or international headquarters, your region and your chapter each have their own hub, connected so that what matters can move between them (06Flow).

People are part of unification

Unification isn't only about tools. It's leadersofficers, committees and members working from the same picture. That's why clear owners for each outcome (01Culture) and one shared scoreboard (08Intelligence) matter as much as one login.

One home doesn't mean everything on every screen. Unification brings everything under one roof; 05Fit decides what shows up in each space.

04 · Ask yourself

Questions for your leadership teamexec board.

  1. How many apps does a member need to stay fully involved in a typical month?
  2. How many logins does a new leaderofficer inherit?
  3. Where does a decision made in a meeting get recorded, and who sees it?
  4. What do leadersofficers copy by hand from one tool to another?
  5. Which app would members miss least if it disappeared tomorrow?
