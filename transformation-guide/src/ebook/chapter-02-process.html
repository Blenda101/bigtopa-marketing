Chapter 02 · Floor · Governed

Process

Rules aren't the opposite of community. They're what lets people relax.

How do we make decisions and keep the space safe?

01 · What breaks without it

When nobody knows the rules, the loudest voice sets them.

Group chats have no governing documentsbylaws. Anyone can post anything, nobody is clearly in charge of keeping the peace, and when a thread turns ugly the only tools are leaving the group or deleting the chat. LeadersOfficers end up moderating by private text message, inconsistently and late at night.

Decisions suffer too. Votes happen in a thread half the members never saw, results live in someone's notes, and the next leadership teamexec board can't find what was decided or why.

Without processWhat members feel
No posted guidelinesUnsure what's okay to say
Moderation by private textRules depend on who's watching

One set of rules for every spaceEither too strict for socializing or too loose for official business

02 · What good looks like

Clear rules, fair moderation, decisions on the record.

Healthy groups write their rules down, apply them the same way every time, and keep a record. Good process is mostly invisible: members feel it as calm, not as policing.

  • Clear guidelinesPosted where members can find them, in plain language, matching how the space is actually set up.
  • Consistent responseThe same report gets the same response, whoever is on duty.
  • Hide, don't deleteProblem posts are hidden from members but kept for review, so decisions can be checked later.
  • EscalationModerators handle the everyday. Leadership handles the serious.

Moderation that fits the space

A social space, an official business meeting and a program for young members need different rules. Moderation should adapt to each space and audience instead of applying one blunt setting everywhere. It also keeps the space tidy, archiving dead rooms and stale threads so clutter doesn't build up.

Private features need guardrails too. Side conversations such as notes, whispers and huddles can be reported to moderators, nothing private is recorded without everyone's consent, and in spaces with members under 18, private one-on-one features between adults and minors are turned off.

Roles with the right reach

Not everyone needs the same powers. Admins set policy, organizers and facilitators run their spaces, and moderators keep community and event spaces healthy. In organizations with local chapters, the parent organization can set moderation templates that local groups inherit.Your national or international headquarters can set moderation templates that chapters inherit, the same way bylaws flow down.

03 · How BigTopaGreekHouse delivers it

The Moderation Mixer.

Moderation works like a sound mixer: sliders for content, behavior, privacy and community standards, each set in clear steps rather than by a hidden algorithm. Set it once for a space and it applies consistently, whoever is on duty.

  • Space roles and permissionsOwners and admins control who can create rooms, invite members and publish.
  • Moderation toolsHide posts, mute or remove participants, and review reports, with nothing deleted outright.
  • Moderation MixerSliders that set how strict each space is, with steps you can explain to members.
  • Generated guidelinesCommunity guidelines written from your mixer settings and saved where members can find them.
  • Templates passed downThe parent organization setsYour national or international headquarters sets moderation templates; local groupschapters inherit them.

04 · Ask yourself

Questions for your leadership teamexec board.

  1. Where are your group's rules written down, and could a new member find them in under a minute?
  2. Who handles a complaint at 11 p.m. on a Saturday?
  3. When a post crosses the line, is it hidden and recorded, or deleted and forgotten?
  4. Does your official business space follow different rules than your social space? Should it?
  5. Can next year's leadership teamexec board find what this year's leadership teamexec board decided, and why?

05 · One signal to watch

Time to resolve a report.

↓

The typical time between a member reporting a problem and a moderator resolving it. When it falls, members can trust that someone is watching. When it rises, it's an early warning, before members start leaving quietly.
